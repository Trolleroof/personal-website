Heat illness is a real problem for the Army. In 2024 it recorded 36.4 heat strokes and 183.9 heat-exhaustion cases per 100,000 person-years, and heat exhaustion has gone up every year since 2020. A medic watching a unit train still has to judge most of this by eye. Wearables can see heart rate, skin temperature, workload, and the weather. Nobody in the field can see core temperature, which is the number that actually means heat stroke.

[SoldierPhysio](https://trussforum.org/model-evaluations/soldierphysio-bench) puts 15 frontier models in that medic's seat. Each one gets the same cases and has to do three things: decide whether to keep a soldier training or pull them out, apply the Army heat chart (TB MED 507) to a real soldier, and try to beat the Army's existing estimate of core temperature.

The full evaluation, with scores and cost for every model, is on Truss.
